QUESTIONS FAMILIES ASK

Frequently Asked Questions

Do we have to start at Station 01?

Most families begin with the SMI Awareness Factor™, because every later station builds on it. In your first conversation, SMI looks at where your family is today and recommends the right starting station.

How long does the journey take?

The SMI Continuity Journey™ is a guided 12-week program. Every family moves at its own pace, and in your first conversation SMI looks at where your family is today and recommends the right starting station.

Does SMI replace our attorney?

No. SMI guides your family in crafting its governance documents. Your family's own attorney reviews and approves them, and then the family formally ratifies them (Station 04).

Which governance documents will our family have?

The Family Constitution, Family Charter, Family Policies and Family Council Manual, crafted with SMI's guidance in Governance Architecture (Station 03).

Can the next generation be involved?

Yes. The journey is built around the whole family, and Succession Readiness (Station 05) focuses on preparing and mentoring next-generation leaders.

Do you work with families outside the United States?

SMI serves family enterprises across diverse cultural and generational contexts. Programs are currently delivered in English and Spanish, with Italian and Arabic planned within the next two years. Legacy Continuity (Station 06) includes support for business expansion and investment beyond borders.

GOVERNANCE

The Four Documents Every Family Enterprise Needs

When a family business grows, good intentions are no longer enough. Decisions need a clear home, and that home is written governance.

The Family Constitution sets out who the family is: its values, its purpose and the principles that guide how it treats one another and the enterprise.

The Family Charter describes how the family and the business relate: who may work in the business, how leaders are chosen and how ownership is handled.

Family Policies turn those principles into everyday rules, such as employment, compensation, conflict resolution and the use of family assets.

The Family Council Manual explains how the family meets, decides and keeps records, so the work continues even as people change.

In Governance Architecture (Station 03), SMI guides the family in crafting all four documents. The family's own attorney then reviews them before the family ratifies them.

GOVERNANCE

What Ratification Means for Your Family

A governance document only matters if the family truly owns it. That is the purpose of legal approval and ratification (Station 04).

First, the family's own attorney reviews the documents SMI helped craft. The attorney makes sure they are legally sound and fit the family's situation and the laws that apply.

Then the family formally ratifies them. Ratification is the moment each member agrees, together, that these are the rules the family will live by.

This step turns good intentions into commitments. It also protects relationships, because future decisions point back to what everyone already accepted, not to one person's memory or preference.

With ratified governance in place, the family is ready for the next station: preparing the next generation to lead.

SUCCESSION

Preparing the Next Generation to Lead

Many founders hope their children will carry the business forward, yet surveys show many young people do not want to join the family enterprise. Preparation makes the difference.

In Succession Readiness (Station 05), the work follows what the family already decided in its ratified governance documents. That keeps the process fair and clear for everyone.

The family identifies and assesses possible successors against agreed criteria, then develops and mentors them over time.

Roles and a transition timeline are defined, and knowledge and key relationships are passed on step by step, with the Family Council overseeing the transition.

The goal is a next generation that is ready, willing and trusted to lead, so what the founder built can continue beyond the founder.

GLOBAL OPPORTUNITY

Thinking Beyond Borders

For many families, continuity also means opportunity in other markets. Legacy Continuity (Station 06) supports the next generation as they lead the business and look beyond borders.

Expansion starts with a strong home base: clear governance, prepared leaders and an enterprise that does not depend on one person.

From there, families can explore business expansion and investment abroad, access to international markets and, where appropriate, legal residency or citizenship through investment.

Each step is taken carefully and with the family's trusted legal and financial advisors, because decisions abroad affect both the enterprise and the family.

The result SMI works toward is a resilient, diversified family enterprise that endures across generations and nations.
